# Udyoga Pramoda

## Making progress visible in a volunteer-driven career community

Udyoga Pramoda started as a community platform for **jobs, events, classes and mentors**.

The platform could help young people discover opportunities and connect with working professionals. But I noticed a problem after people joined: **many participants did not consistently attend classes or complete the work given to them.**

For a volunteer-driven mentor community, that created a bigger problem.

Mentors were giving their time to help people grow, but there was little visibility into **who was actually learning, who was putting in consistent effort, and who needed attention.**

I redesigned the product around one idea:

> **Make progress visible to the people who can act on it.**

# 01 — Where it started

## Udyoga Pramoda already had the pieces of a career ecosystem

The first version wasn't trying to compete with every career platform.

It brought together things that were already happening within the community:

**Jobs** — People could discover opportunities.

**Events & classes** — The community could conduct sessions, hackathons and skill-building programs.

**Mentors** — Young people could browse working professionals and send connection requests.

**Community communication** — WhatsApp groups helped people communicate outside the product.

Mentors were professionals volunteering their time to help younger people develop skills. They could accept or reject connection requests, guide mentees and share relevant opportunities from their professional networks.

So the basic journey already existed:

**Discover → Connect → Learn → Find opportunities**

The problem wasn't access.

It was what happened **after someone joined.**

---

# 02 — The problem I noticed

## Participation was visible. Commitment wasn't.

Through my involvement in the community, I noticed that people would often join classes or programs but stop participating midway.

Some attended regularly. Some completed the work. Others joined but gradually disappeared.

For a normal content platform, that might simply look like low engagement.

But Udyoga Pramoda was different because the people providing the guidance were **volunteers**.

A mentor might spend their limited free time teaching a class or helping a mentee. If the mentee wasn't following through, the mentor had little evidence that their effort was translating into progress.

And the admin had an even broader problem:

> **Who is actually progressing?**

A registration told them someone joined.

A WhatsApp group told them someone was present.

Neither necessarily showed **consistent effort**.

---

# 03 — The opportunity

## What if the platform could show the work happening between connection and opportunity?

I didn't want to turn Udyoga Pramoda into another learning-management system.

The goal was to add a lightweight progression layer to the community that already existed.

The core loop became:

**Mentor gives guidance → Mentor assigns a task → Mentee completes the task → Mentee submits the work → Mentor reviews it → Progress becomes visible**

This created a new signal:

> **Not just “Did this person join?” but “Are they actually putting in the effort?”**

---

# 04 — Why visible progress matters

## The same progress signal helps three sides of the community

The redesign wasn't just about giving mentees a task list. It made progress useful to **everyone involved**.

### Mentee

**“What should I do next?”**

Tasks turn vague mentoring into concrete action.

### Mentor

**“Is this person actually progressing?”**

Completed work gives mentors evidence they can use when deciding how to guide the mentee, what to teach next, and whether they are ready to recommend an opportunity.

### Admin

**“Where is the community actually moving?”**

Admins can see more than registrations and identify people who are consistently putting in effort, even before a placement happens.

---

# 05 — Why this makes Udyoga Pramoda different

## A job board measures opportunities. Udyoga Pramoda can represent the journey toward them.

Platforms such as LinkedIn or Naukri are primarily designed around:

**Profile → Job → Application → Hiring**

Udyoga Pramoda serves a different part of the journey.

Its value is in the space between:

**“I want a job.”**

and

**“I'm ready for this opportunity.”**

The platform brings together:

**Learning + Mentorship + Effort + Progress + Opportunities**

Someone doesn't need to already have a strong professional profile to get value from the platform.

They can **build evidence of their effort inside the community.**

And importantly, **placement isn't the only measure of progress.**

Someone might not get placed immediately. But if they attend sessions, connect with mentors, complete tasks, improve their skills and consistently participate, the platform can make that effort visible.

> **Udyoga Pramoda helps a community recognise people who are actively becoming opportunity-ready.**

# 06 — Designing the mentee experience

## From “What does this platform offer?” to “What should I do next?”

The redesigned mentee dashboard brings the user's current activity into one place.

The experience surfaces:

- Progress
- Tasks
- Mentor connections
- Upcoming sessions
- Events
- Job opportunities

The goal was not to add more information.

It was to establish a clearer hierarchy:

> **Progress first. Opportunity alongside it.**

### The task flow

1. **Mentor assigns a task**
2. **Mentee receives it in My Tasks**
3. **Mentee completes and submits the work**
4. **Mentor reviews it**
5. **Mentor validates or provides direction**

This creates a shared state between the two people instead of leaving the entire progression inside WhatsApp.

---

# 07 — Designing for mentors

## Mentors are volunteers. The product should respect their time.

The mentor dashboard was designed around the things mentors actually need to act on.

### Mentees needing attention

Mentors can see people whose work needs review or validation.

### Progress

They can understand where a mentee currently stands instead of reconstructing the journey from conversations.

### Sessions

Upcoming mentoring activity stays visible.

### Opportunities

Mentors can contribute relevant job opportunities to the wider community.

The design principle was:

> **Don't turn volunteering into administration. Surface the decisions that matter.**

---

# 08 — Designing for admins

## Admins needed visibility across the community, not another spreadsheet.

Udyoga Pramoda's organisers conduct events, hackathons and skill-building programs.

Their challenge isn't simply publishing an event. It's understanding what happens afterward.

The redesigned system gives them a way to see participation through activity and progress.

That helps answer:

- Who is actively participating?
- Who is completing assigned work?
- Who may need additional support?
- Which participants are consistently engaged?
- Who is progressing even if they haven't reached a placement yet?

This changes the admin's role from **tracking registrations** to **understanding community progression**.

---

# 09 — The system

```text
DISCOVER
Jobs / Events
      ↓
CONNECT
Mentor
      ↓
LEARN
Guidance / Classes
      ↓
ACT
Mentor-assigned task
      ↓
COMPLETE
Submit work
      ↓
REVIEW
Mentor validates
      ↓
PROGRESS
Effort becomes visible
      ↓
OPPORTUNITY
Jobs / Referrals
```

The important part is that **progress becomes a shared signal**.

It helps the mentor decide where to invest time and helps the admin understand what is happening across the community.

---

# 10 — Why I chose role-specific experiences

The three users weren't solving the same problem.

### Mentee
**“What do I need to do next?”**

### Mentor
**“Who needs my attention?”**

### Admin
**“Who is progressing?”**

Instead of giving everyone the same dashboard, I designed around these different mental models.

---

# 11 — What we actually shipped

This wasn't a large product team.

I worked with **one developer** to build the initial V1 product and then redesigned the experience based on what I observed in the community.

The redesign was developed and shipped as the next product direction.

However:

**The redesigned UI had not yet been updated on the public website.**

So I don't present this as “the redesigned website went live.”

The product work, flows and implementation were completed, while the public-facing website still showed the earlier visual experience.

# 13 — What changed

### Before

**Jobs + Events + Mentors + Community**

The platform helped people find and connect.

### After the redesign

**Jobs + Events + Mentors + Tasks + Progress**

The platform could also represent what happened **after the connection**.

> **From being a place where opportunities were posted to a place where a community could see people moving toward those opportunities.**

---

# 14 — What I learned

The biggest lesson from Udyoga Pramoda was that **engagement is not the same as progress.**

A person joining an event is an activity.

A person completing the work, returning to their mentor, improving their skills and continuing the journey is progress.

That distinction changed how I thought about the product.

I stopped looking at the platform as:

> **“How do we give people more opportunities?”**

and started looking at it as:

> **“How do we help the community recognise and support the people who are actually working toward those opportunities?”**

That became the foundation of the redesign.

## The idea in one line

> **Udyoga Pramoda doesn't just connect young people to opportunities. It makes the effort between learning and opportunity visible.**
