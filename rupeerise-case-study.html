THE STARTING POINT

The original assumption was the obvious one

The hackathon brief was financial literacy. The working theory: if people understood money, investing, or financial instruments better, they'd make better decisions.

So V1 focused on learning and managing money — education, budgeting, savings, investing, market information, mentorship, quizzes.

Learn→Manage→Invest→Get guidance
RESEARCH

Two directional signals, from ten conversations

~10 informal 1:1 interviews with Gen Z and Millennials, in person at a café. Some were already investing, some knew about investing, some were actively trading. Unrecorded and exploratory — treated as directional, not representative. The two groups below may overlap, so they aren't treated as separate populations.

≥6 OF 10

Information was already everywhere

Finfluencer content left people with partial, fragmented knowledge — while they were still trying to make real decisions.

≥4 OF 10

Deciding was still hard

FOMO, overtrading, difficulty judging risk. At this point I didn't change direction — I presented the original V1 product as planned. This insight became important later.

THE TURNING POINT

The jury asked the question that mattered most

V1 shipped as a functional MVP — rudimentary UI, AI-generated finance quizzes, presented to the hackathon jury. Feedback was positive. They saw potential. But they raised one hard question:

"Why would someone come back?"

One-time quiz completion isn't a reason to return. That question — not a redesign brief — is what sent me back to the interview data.

REFRAMING

Knowing isn't the same as applying

Knowing what a stock, mutual fund, or credit card is doesn't mean someone can apply that knowledge when making a real decision. I didn't replace the financial-literacy problem — I extended it:

How might RupeeRise help people turn financial knowledge into more deliberate financial decisions?
Learn→Understand→Simulate→Decide→Reflect
WHO IT'S FOR

The need changes with experience

A younger user needs more learning. Someone already investing needs more help with decision-making and reflection. The product is scoped for early-stage financial learners across these three bands, with the balance shifting from foundational learning toward decision-making as experience grows.

12–18

Financial awareness and supervision. Parental controls were on the future roadmap — not built in V2.

18–25

Building financial confidence and decision habits.

25–35

Already financially active — benefits most from understanding their own patterns.

SIMULATION LAB

Practice, before real money is at risk

Users make investment decisions against market scenarios without immediately risking real money. The point isn't to make investing more exciting — it's a place to practise how they respond to uncertainty.

THESIS LOG

The friction is intentional

I considered making reflection optional — faster, but easier to skip. Instead, before acting, the user answers: why this position, what's expected, what would trigger an exit.

Impulse→Action
Impulse→Reflection→Action

The thesis also creates something the system can compare against the eventual outcome — the user's reasoning becomes part of the learning loop.

The thesis isn't graded on entry. Its value comes from comparing stated reasoning against what actually happened, later, in Behavioral Insights — the friction is there for reflection, not to produce another instant score.

STABILITY SCORE

Score the decision, not the return

This is the decision the case study hangs on — the A/B comparison at the top. A return-based system ranks Decision A higher. But A could have been lucky, and B may have been the more disciplined call. So the score reads risk awareness, diversification, consistency, discipline, and reaction patterns instead of profit.

I considered showing several separate behavioral scores — more detail, but harder to parse quickly. I chose one overall score as the entry point, with the underlying behaviors explaining what changed.

How am I making decisions?

— not "how much money did I make?"

There's a tension here I don't think fully resolves: a metric is still something users can learn to optimize, even a well-chosen one. The mitigation is that the score moves gradually and every change is explained through behavioral signals rather than delivered as an instant reward.

Free tier

Capital Discipline Score at 82/100, with each contributing behavior shown separately — emergency-fund runway, goal consistency — and each stating exactly how many points it contributed and why. This is the "underlying behaviors explaining what changed" principle made concrete, one layer below the single Stability Score.

BEHAVIORAL INSIGHTS

Help users understand their own patterns

A normal dashboard shows returns and market movement. It doesn't show how a person behaves. V2 connects personal activity with market context to surface patterns:

During previous periods of market hype, your trading activity increased significantly.

The product isn't telling the user what to buy — it's helping them understand how they tend to behave. Instead of predicting the market for the user, RupeeRise helps the user understand themselves.

Premium

Risk Intelligence: behavior pattern, correctly, next to market forecast

"During previous market hype cycles, your trade frequency increased 210% vs. baseline" is exactly the behavioral-insight promise, done well. The stress-test panel below it predates the copy decision to frame this as historical comparison rather than forecast — see tension 02.

AI AS A LEARNING LAYER

Explain the pattern, don't predict the market

AI was already used in V1 to generate quizzes and puzzles. For V2, the role expands: user, portfolio, and market metadata become inputs for more contextual feedback. AI could help explain —

  • Why a Stability Score changed
  • What pattern appeared in recent decisions
  • How market conditions relate to a user's behavior
  • What the user could learn from a previous decision

AI would not make the investment decision for the user — it supports learning and reflection. That distinction matters because most financial products are moving toward AI-driven market predictions. RupeeRise goes the other way:

Don't predict the market for me. Help me predict my own behavior.

This broader AI capability was a V2 direction — not integrated into the four-day hackathon MVP.

BEHAVIORAL GUARDRAILS

The product shouldn't reward more trading

The principle: protect the user's decision-making, don't optimize for more transactions. Deliberately avoided:

Trading streaks
Rewards frequency, not judgment.
Profit leaderboards
Turns outcomes into competition, reintroducing profit-as-proxy.
Trending-stock feeds
Increases FOMO — the exact behavior the product works against.
Engagement rewards for frequent trading
Drives activity, not discipline.
Unnecessary price alerts
Manufactures urgency where none exists.

Instead, if trading frequency suddenly spikes above a user's normal pattern, the product surfaces a warning or adjusts behavioral guidance — it responds to signals rather than chasing engagement.

WHY NOT ZERODHA OR GROWW

A different problem, not a better feed

Those products help users manage and execute investments. RupeeRise helps users learn concepts, practise decisions, record reasoning, understand patterns, and build habits over time.

Investing apps help me manage my money. RupeeRise helps me understand how I manage myself.
BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY — FUTURE, NOT VALIDATED

Behavioral signals could become useful to institutions too

With user consent, real financial activity could make the behavioral system more useful — and give banks or investment platforms a way to understand customer readiness before recommending products.

This ties back to the core differentiation: an investment platform owns the transaction. RupeeRise is designed to understand the behavior around the transaction — that's the layer a Zerodha or Groww can't build without becoming a different kind of company.

Deeper interpretation of the same behavior, not new features

Premium's job is to deepen the same behavioral value, not turn RupeeRise into another trading app: deeper historical pattern analysis, more personalized interpretation of how a user's behavior compares to past market periods or their own past decisions. A forward-looking stress test is framed as a learning comparison, not a market prediction. The success metric isn't more investing activity — it's whether users become more deliberate and consistent in how they decide.

KNOWN TENSIONS

One open, two resolved in writing — screens haven't caught up yet

Same standard as the rest of this write-up: no smoothing over.

01 — OPEN

Tier downgrade reads as punishment

"Tier Adjustment Alert," a struck-through Tier 3 dropping to Tier 2, "violates Tier 3 consistency rules" — this is the exact pattern rejected elsewhere: inform, don't punish. The mechanism (adjusting exposure limits after detected instability) is sound; the framing needs the same "what changed, why, what to improve" treatment as the Stability Score gets everywhere else.

LIMITATIONS

What ten conversations can and can't tell you

Exploratory, unrecorded, ~10 people. The "≥6" and "≥4" groups may overlap — not separate populations.

CAN SAY
  • ≥6 participants recognized fragmented finfluencer knowledge
  • ≥4 described FOMO, overtrading, or risk-judgment difficulty
  • Some participants were already investing or trading
CAN'T SAY
  • That all young investors face these problems
  • That the Stability Score changes real financial behavior
  • That users would pay for premium
  • That institutions would adopt the business model
WHAT I LEARNED
Financial literacy isn't only about knowing concepts. It's about applying them when a real decision is in front of you.

That shift changed RupeeRise from a collection of learning features into a system: Learn → Practise → Decide → Reflect → Improve. The question the jury asked is still the one I'd need to validate first — why would someone come back? V2's answer isn't another stock prediction or more trading. It's the chance to understand how they make decisions, recognize their patterns, and build better habits.

RupeeRise isn't trying to predict the market for the user. It's trying to help the user understand themselves.
