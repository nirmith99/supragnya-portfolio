Context

The hackathon brief asked us to close financial literacy gaps among Millennials and Gen Z. It's a well-worn prompt—most teams would reach for it and build another learn-and-earn app. Before committing to a direction, I wanted to understand if literacy was actually the problem.

What the Research Actually Said

Informal interviews with 20–28 year olds didn't surface ignorance — they surfaced behavioral contradiction. These were people who could explain credit card interest, who'd watched crypto crash documentaries, who knew the theory. And yet they were panic selling, trend-chasing, and misusing credit under pressure.

Patterns Found in Research

  • Credit misuse despite knowing the math
  • Overconfidence after short-term wins
  • Trend-driven investing, not conviction-driven
  • Panic selling during volatility

Synthesis: The problem was never knowledge — it was decision stability under pressure. No existing platform was designed to close that gap. Every competitor either gamified engagement or optimized for access. None regulated exposure based on demonstrated behavioral stability.

Reframing the Problem

The research made one thing clear: building another literacy product would miss the actual failure point. So I reframed the brief entirely.

The New HMW

How might we improve financial literacy among young earners?

How might we build financial decision stability in high-access financial environments?

If financial exposure is staged based on demonstrated behavioral stability, reactive financial decisions — and long-term financial damage — can be structurally reduced.

The Stability Score

RupeeRise measures integrated behavioral stability — not knowledge, not returns. The score is a composite of four behavioral inputs, each tracking a different failure mode.

Profit is intentionally excluded. Rewarding profit directly contradicts the behavioral thesis — a lucky user could score well and earn exposure they haven't behaviorally demonstrated.

Volatility Reaction Index

How do you behave when markets move sharply? Panic selling and hype-buying both score low.

Trade Consistency

Are your decisions predictable over time, or erratic and trend-driven?

Capital Discipline Score

Do you meet EMIs, avoid credit overuse, and stay within your cashflow structure?

Diversification Health

Are you concentrated in one bet, or distributing risk meaningfully across asset classes?

Risk Tiers & Exposure Control

Score thresholds map to four tiers. Exposure caps scale dynamically. Real capital is never force-liquidated — new exposure is throttled instead.

Tier 1
Restricted
Simulation only. Foundation building required.
Tier 2
Controlled
Micro-exposure with mandatory thesis logging.
Tier 3
Moderate
Broader access with behavioral guardrails active.
Tier 4
Advanced
Full real exposure mode unlocked at 90%+ stability.

No auto-liquidation. Forced selling punishes users during volatility — exactly when behavioral support is needed most. New exposure is throttled instead; real capital is never force-sold.

Severity-based thresholds, not arbitrary counts. The score doesn't punish a user for three mistakes — it responds to the behavioral severity of each decision relative to their current tier.

Informative friction, not punitive dead-ends. When a Tier 1 user hits their cap, the UI shows exactly how far they are from unlocking, what specific behaviors are holding the score back, and a direct path forward.

Information Architecture

Every module feeds into the Stability Engine — nothing is isolated. The IA is designed so that behavioral data from Foundation, Simulation, and Mentorship all converge into a single score that controls exposure.

Core Modules

The architecture guiding stability, in sequence.

Module 1

Foundation

Before any investing — emergency fund, credit utilization awareness, debt-to-income visibility, cashflow structure. Credit misuse directly dents the Stability Score. Daily financial behavior is investment readiness.

Foundation Layer
Simulation Lab
Module 2

Simulation Lab (0–90%)

Historical volatility scenarios — 2008 crash, 2017 crypto hype, pandemic dips. Every decision flows: Context → Asset Grid → Thesis Log → Stability Impact Feedback. No live trading, no real money.

Module 3

Informative Friction

When a Tier 1 user attempts an action beyond their cap, the UI doesn't show a dead-end. It shows how far away they are from unlocking, what behaviors are holding their score back, and a direct path to improve. The friction is informative, not punitive.

Tier Downgrade Modal
Insights Journal
Mentorship Hub
Module 4

Insights & Mentorship

Free Journal Mode builds macro awareness. Premium Applied Risk Intelligence connects live signals to a user's own exposure history — contextualizing risk before they act on it. Mentorship is not a marketplace. Users must complete a reflection before booking. Intentional friction.

Module 5

Real Exposure Mode

Unlocks post 90% Stability Score + Tier 4 eligibility. Premium only. Same behavioral principles from simulation — now with real capital. Exposure caps, thesis logging, and intervention logic remain active throughout.

Real Exposure Mode

Ethical Guardrails

A behavioral finance product can easily become its own harm. We designed around the most common failure modes in fintech UX.

🚫

No Dopamine Loops

No streaks, confetti, or reward mechanics tied to trading frequency.

📵

No Trending Feeds

No "hot stocks" section. No real-time social sentiment. Context, not noise.

🚫

No Influencer Layer

Mentorship is structured discipline reinforcement — not a marketplace of opinion.

⚖️

No Force Liquidation

Real capital is never auto-sold. New exposure is throttled; existing positions are protected.

📊

Severity-Based Intervention

Interventions are proportional to behavioral severity — not a punishment counter.

💡

Stability > Profit

Profit is intentionally excluded from scoring. This is the core ethical stance.

Outcome & What I'd Do Differently

Market-ready concept — requires security hardening before production.

What I Learned

The shift from "how do we teach people about money" to "how do we structurally reduce behavioral damage" is not a small pivot. It changed the entire design model — from content delivery to behavioral gating. That reframe was the most important decision we made, and it only happened because we spent time in research before opening Figma.

The 14-day constraint also forced prioritization in a healthy way. Every feature had to earn its place inside the stability hypothesis. Anything that didn't connect to the behavioral architecture got cut.

! Future Scope

  • Calibrate score weightings with real behavioral data from beta users.
  • Validate severity thresholds via controlled user testing.
  • Design for the edge case: the stable user with an urgent capital need.
  • Build the compliance and security layer required for real exposure mode.
